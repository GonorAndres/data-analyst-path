# Insurance reserving: evidence and decision brief

**Analyst:** Andres Gonzalez Ortega · **Reviewed:** 2026-09-05
**Source:** CAS Schedule P, accident years 1988–1997; separate synthetic claim-level illustrations.

## Decision

How does development affect projected unpaid losses, and which assumptions need investigation before selecting a reserve?

## Evidence

The triangle follows cumulative paid or incurred losses by accident year and development lag. The published case includes a dated API snapshot; local processed source tables were unavailable for independent reproduction during this review. The earlier ~$20.4M headline and unconditional profitability rankings are withdrawn until monetary units, selected companies, valuation date, and output totals are reconciled.

| Quantity | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Paid Chain-Ladder ultimate minus latest paid | Projected unpaid loss, including case reserves and IBNR |
| Incurred Chain-Ladder ultimate minus reported incurred | Estimated IBNR on that method's basis |
| Combined ratio | Loss ratio plus **assumed 30% expense ratio** |
| Claim severity, frequency, and reporting lag | Simulated individual claims, not observed policyholder records |

## Interpretation

Chain-Ladder extrapolates observed development. Bornhuetter-Ferguson incorporates an expected loss assumption. Their difference is a sensitivity comparison, not a confidence interval, and either may be higher. A low loss ratio alone does not establish underwriting profitability; expenses and the valuation basis matter.

## Proposed next steps

1. Reproduce aggregates and verify currency scaling against the original Schedule P files.
2. Assess company mix, development stability, tail factors, and expense-ratio sensitivity.
3. Separate observed aggregate evidence from simulated claim explanations in every presentation.
4. Quantify uncertainty before making reserve, pricing, or capital recommendations.
